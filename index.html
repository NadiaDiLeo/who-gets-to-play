    WHAT I STUDY

    Physical accessibility

    Can a player perceive and operate the game? How is the user experience influenced by subtitles, remappable controls, readable text, and alternative inputs?

    Conceptual accessibility

    Does the game, and the culture around it, make room for the player? We will analyze how players present their identities through avatars, how much they value feeling represented, and how community norms enforced by online spaces influence accessibility.

    Where I look

    Serious games, meaning games with an explicit educational purpose that are not meant to be played mainly for amusement (Abt, 1970), and the commercial games they get compared with. In my view, though, any game can become serious, whether or not it was designed for that. James Paul Gee makes a similar point about commercial games, which he calls “learning machines” (Gee, 2003). Minecraft is my own example: a commercial game that ended up in classrooms (Di Leo & Traetta, 2025).

    A GAP IN THE GUIDELINES

    What exists for videogames

    The Game Accessibility Guidelines (GAG) are the most comprehensive resource on game accessibility. They have been written by studios, specialists and academics.

    The guidelines cover about 120 items, sorted by area (motor, cognitive, vision, hearing, speech, general) and by level (basic, intermediate, advanced)

    Nothing equivalent exists for serious games: educational environments have specific requirements that the Guidelines do not cover. Similarly, many of the checks included in the GAGs do not apply to educational games.

    What doesn’t fit a classroom

    • “Offer a means to bypass gameplay elements that aren’t part of the core mechanic”: in a serious game, the part you skip may be the lesson (Habgood & Ainsworth, 2011)
    • GAG picture one player and one game; at school, many settings belong to the teacher

    FROM CHECKLISTS TO PLAYABILITY

    A systematic mapping of “Can I Play That?” and GAGs

    479

    I mapped 479 accessibility reviews (2019–2025) by disabled players onto the GAG (Di Leo & Tarantino, 2026) to understand how accessible mainstream games actually are

    To do so, we manually coded each review and noted whether it mentioned a guideline, and whether it was implemented or missing. Reviews received a composite score based on how many guidelines appeared (represented count) and how many of them were rated positively (positive rate)

    DiGRA 2026

    THE AWARD NOMINEES FOR THE INNOVATION IN ACCESSIBILITY AWARD 2025

    In another study, currently under review, the five nominees for the Innovation in Accessibility award at The Game Awards 2025 were analyzed: Assassin’s Creed Shadows, Atomfall, DOOM: The Dark Ages, EA Sports FC 26, and South of Midnight.

    After a 60-minute playthrough, following the First-Time User Experience framework (Barnett et al., 2018), two annotators (κ = 0.92) evaluated how many GAGs were implemented and documented in each game.

    All of them obtained high scores, but most features were not documented on the games’ websites and many advanced items were still missing.

    DOOM: The Dark Ages won the award while ranking third on compliance. My audit placed South of Midnight at first place.

    GAME CHANGER

    My PhD project: Game changer. Rewriting the rules of inclusive education

    My project involves building a teaching intervention focused on games with specific accessibility settings, above all Minecraft Education.

    I am conducting several Minecraft-based lessons in primary and lower-secondary classes in Italy, with experimental and control groups. The lessons cover creative writing, life science, maths, and environmental education.

    I developed a mixed-methods study design: students’ experiences are evaluated through standardized questionnaires, focus groups, gameplay recordings.

    Through these instruments, I am measuring whether their attitude towards the subjects of the lessons has changed, as well as their interaction with the Minecraft environment.

    Students can pick their own tools and settings, and record those choices throughout the lessons thanks to an accessibility checklist.

    I will use the results to write guidelines for teachers and developers on using games inclusively at school

    WHY MINECRAFT EDUCATION?

    Evidence of its educational effectiveness

    To analyze the impact of Minecraft Education, we conducted a systematic review of 18 peer-reviewed studies (2019–2024), from kindergarten to secondary school (Di Leo & Traetta, 2025)

    We found that Minecraft is used across nations (from Taiwan to Brazil) and school subjects (Geography, Maths, Life Sciences, History etc.). Most studies reported positive results, both qualitatively and quantitatively.

    There are, however, some barriers that need to be addressed, such as the reliance on technological infrastructure and the learning curve of the game, which affects teachers just as much as students.

    IJET 2025

    Di Leo, N., & Traetta, L. (2025). Crafting knowledge block by block: A systematic review on the educational potential of Minecraft in schools. Italian Journal of Educational Technology, 33(1), 67–85.

    EVALUATING MINECRAFT EDUCATION WITH THE GAG

    Evidence of its accessibility

    To assess Minecraft’s accessibility, I manually mapped it to the Game Accessibility Guidelines.

    Minecraft Education fully satisfies 37.2% of the Guidelines, and partially satisfies another 38% of them.

    The General, Speech, and Vision categories are those with the best results, while the Hearing and Motor categories still have some gaps. Further data on Minecraft’s accessibility is being collected thanks to the Accessibility Checklists of the Game Changer project.

    There’s still work to be done, but it confirms itself as a very accessible game.

    WHAT 27,036 REVIEWS SAY

    To get a bigger picture, I analysed 27,036 English reviews from Google Play and the App Store (2018–2026)

    Two coders built a 15-category codebook and manually annotated a sample of the corpus (200 reviews); then, an LLM coded the full corpus (macro-F1 0.84; κ 0.71 vs 0.74 between humans)

    Negative reviews cluster on sign-in, licences and crashes: the gap opens before learning can start

    STRESSING OUT THE DAMSEL IN DISTRESS

    Do gamers feel represented by the characters they play as and included by the communities they inhabit? We conducted 19 semi-structured interviews with gamers, read through the lenses of postdigital feminism (Di Leo et al., 2025)

    Representation

    • Participants saw a shift from hypersexualised women to complex characters such as Samus Aran and Aloy;
    • some participants advocated for female villains who did not necessarily have a later redemption arc.

    Infrastructures

    • Some interviewees avoided using voice chat in multiplayer lobbies, as it often reveals the gender of who uses it, so women muted their mics or picked male nicknames to avoid abuse;
    • Some interviewees believed that Twitch rewards a narrow, hyper-feminine self-presentation.

    Practices

    • There were some complaints about “forced representation” (citing the Sweet Baby Inc. company: ‘they forced ideologies into games (the so-called woke), aiming to change the plot or dialogues.’), together with examples of meaningful change (Celeste as an example of a subtle, effective portrayal of a queer protagonist)
    • Heterogeneous development teams were the fix participants kept returning to

    Di Leo, N., Rizzi, A., & Traetta, L. (2025). Stressing out the ‘damsel in distress’: Intersectional shifts in women’s representation in video games. Postdigital Science and Education, 8, 113–139.

    THE BACKLASH

    GamerGate noticed us (and proved our point)!

    • A post about our article on the subreddit r/KotakuInAction gained some traction (123 upvotes, 46 comments).
    • Commenters went after women participants’ “low” play times and our supposed politics
    • There were also insults and body-shaming
    • This confirmed what the participants had told us: gatekeeping and hostility towards women are sadly common in male gaming spaces
    • GamerGate began in 2014 around women in games, and went on to target academics who work in Game Studies
